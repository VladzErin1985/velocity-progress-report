KONSIER — TIER-ROUTING PIECE AUDITED, SIX FAULTS FOUND AND FIXED (KS-A2): This is the piece every qualified inbound lead runs through — it sorts the lead into a plan tier (Starter, Professional, or Enterprise) by expected call volume, moves the deal to the right stage, sets the monthly value on it, and posts a tiered alert to the team. Switched on since May, never once run against a real lead.

Went through it end to end. All five team-alert steps were missing a required setting and would have failed outright. There was no failure-alert safety net wired in at all. A blank or malformed trigger would have been read as a valid mid-tier lead — pushing a real deal into the “qualified” stage, attaching a made-up monthly value, and posting a confident alert, with nothing flagging it. A strict number check could crash the piece before its own safeguards ran. The duplicate-check could crash on a failed lookup. And the trigger left the calling piece hanging with no reply if any step failed.

Fixed all six. Added a valid-input gate that blocks any CRM change and raises a review alert when the trigger data is bad, a visible note when a manual override skips routing, and a “no call-volume data, verify the tier” flag on the alert for leads that get defaulted. Fifteen steps to eighteen, clean. Still waiting on real inbound traffic to prove it, same as the rest of the launch pieces. The pipeline-destination question for this piece is still a Drew decision.

KONSIER — ONBOARDING RUNBOOK WRITTEN UP AS A WORKING DOCUMENT: Took the onboarding model from the day before and turned it into the actual step-by-step runbook the team will follow — the 9 gated stages each with a named owner, a fill-in tracking sheet to complete per customer (which instance, which phone number, which CRM, the rollback step, the human-transfer contact), and the vertical-specific additions: the medical-privacy note for dental and medical, the no-legal-advice note for legal, estimate-only language for the trades. Also worked out the full tool split — which accounts and connections the customer provides on their side (their phone number, their CRM, their calendar) versus which ones Konsier owns and runs on its own server and never hands over (the automation backend, the voice-platform account, the CRM that tracks the sales and onboarding pipeline). Still a document, not a build.

KONSIER — REPLY-TRIAGE PIECE AUDITED, THREE EXPOSED KEYS AND A SILENT DUPLICATE BUG FOUND AND FIXED (KS-WF1): This is the piece that catches every reply to a Konsier cold outbound email, reads it, sorts it into interested / objection / question / unsubscribe / auto-reply, and routes each one — the interested ones get a CRM record and a callback, unsubscribes get suppressed. Switched on since May, never once triggered by a real reply.

Went through it end to end. Found three live service keys sitting in plain text inside the piece — the AI service, the email platform, and the calling platform — moved all three into the shared secure vault. Found a real silent bug in the duplicate-check: it was looking up the wrong record, so it could never actually match, which means a repeated or re-sent reply would have created a second duplicate contact, a second deal, and a second outbound call every time, with nothing flagging it. Fixed the lookup so the check works.

Closed two real gaps in what the piece does with a warm reply. Before, an objection or a question only posted a quick team notification and then stopped — nothing was written to the CRM, so a missed notification meant a lost lead. Now every objection and question creates a real contact record with the reply saved as a note. And before, if someone who already replied once replied again — usually a stronger buying signal — the piece treated it as a duplicate and dropped it silently. Now a repeat reply adds a dated note to their existing record and raises a proper “they replied again, follow up” alert.

Rounded out with the standard closeout: the failure-alert safety net wired in, retries added on every outside call, and the piece's own description brought up to date. Nothing here changes activation — it still waits on the outbound campaign going live for whichever sector gets picked.

KONSIER — CLIENT ONBOARDING RUNBOOK + THE DEDICATED CUSTOMER BACKEND MAPPED OUT: Put together the working model for how a new Konsier customer gets set up, checked against how already-scaled AI-receptionist and automation companies actually run this. The onboarding is a 9-step gated sequence — intake, scope and guardrails, knowledge build, phone and system connections, internal testing, customer sign-off, launch with a tested rollback, support handoff, first-week review — each step with a clear owner and no promise of a same-day launch. The intake list covers everything needed from the customer up front: business details and hours, services and prices, the call types and the intended outcome for each, escalation triggers, their existing phone number and carrier, their calendar and CRM, and the recording-consent rules for their state.

Also worked out the dedicated customer backend Drew asked for: one isolated automation instance per customer, all running on a single server Konsier owns and controls, each customer walled off from the others, with the customer only ever seeing their own results dashboard, never the build itself. That is the mechanism that stops a customer from taking the system in-house and dropping the subscription. Wrote all of this up as a reference document — a plan on paper, not built yet.

KONSIER — VOICE RECEPTIONIST SCRIPTS AUDITED, FOUNDER NAME REMOVED (ALEX + GRACE): Went through both AI receptionist scripts end to end — Alex, the one already live and taking real calls, and the second one still being finalized for the professional-services side. These are staged and ready — live callers aren't affected until they're switched on.
